📣 Marketing case study

Start with why: how Apple sells a computer by leading with a belief

From “How Great Leaders Inspire Action” · Simon Sinek · TED · 18 min

A marketing case study of Simon Sinek's TED talk. Every quote and number links to the moment in the video.

The hook

“People don't buy what you do; people buy why you do it.”

4:33
Sinek repeats this line throughout the talk. It's the thesis of the whole case.

The puzzle

Same talent, same agencies, different results

  • Apple has “the same access to the same talent, the same agencies” as everyone else 0:40
  • The Wright brothers beat teams that were “better qualified, better funded” 1:10
  • Sinek's claim: inspiring leaders all communicate the same way, the opposite of everyone else 1:47
Set up the mystery: resources don't explain who wins.

The insight

The golden circle: most brands talk outside in

  1. WhatEveryone knows what they do, 100 percent 2:22
  2. HowSome know how: the USP or proprietary process 2:29
  3. WhyVery few know why. Not profit: “That's a result” 2:36
Inspired organizations think, act and communicate from the inside out, starting with why (3:05).

Same facts, reversed order, a different reaction

Outside in

“We make great computers… Want to buy one?” 3:28

Inside out

“Everything we do, we believe in challenging the status quo… We just happen to make great computers.” 4:08

“I just reversed the order of the information.” (4:31)

The proof

Competitors could build it. Nobody bought it.

  • Gateway launched flat-screen TVs. “Nobody bought one.” 5:05
  • Dell launched MP3 players and PDAs. “Nobody bought one.” 5:17
  • Yet people happily buy phones and MP3 players from a computer company 4:47
The product capability was there. The belief wasn't.

Langley had the “recipe for success”. The Wrights had a why.

$50,000from the War Department to Langley
0college degrees on the Wright team
5sets of parts per test day
Funding (8:51), no college education (9:33), five sets of parts because they'd crash that many times before supper (10:17).

15–18%

market penetration before an idea tips into the mass market, per the law of diffusion of innovation

11:45
Innovators (2.5%) and early adopters (13.5%) buy on belief; the majority waits for them (11:12).

TiVo led with features, and the majority said no

What TiVo said

“We have a product that pauses live TV, skips commercials…” 14:32

What Sinek suggests

“If you're the kind of person who likes to have total control… boy, do we have a product for you.” 14:51

Highest-quality product, well funded, great market conditions, and still a commercial failure (13:48).
He gave the “I have a dream” speech, not the “I have a plan” speech.

Simon Sinek 17:07

250,000 people showed up in 1963 with no invitations (15:21). They came for what they believed.

Why this works for marketers

Features answer “what”. A belief gives early adopters a reason to choose you, and something to repeat to others. Lead with the belief, then let the product be the proof.

Our interpretation, not Sinek's words.

What to steal

Three moves for your next campaign

🧭

Open with your why

State the belief first, then how, then what.

🎯

Target believers

Sell to people who believe what you believe, not everyone who needs it.

🧾

Make products the proof

“What you do simply proves what you believe.”

Quote on the third card is from 13:08.

Your turn 🤔

Your new app has great features but low signups. What would Sinek change first?

  • A. Add more features to the landing page
  • B. Lead the page with what you believe, then the features
  • C. Cut the price

Sinek's answer: B. Reverse the order of the information 4:31
