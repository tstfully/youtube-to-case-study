# Case Study Frameworks

> **When to read this:** Step 3, after you've extracted the facts from the transcript. Pick one framework and follow its arc. Each one maps to slides.

A case study is a **story with evidence**: someone faced a problem, made a choice, and something happened. The reader should finish knowing *what* happened, *why* it worked (or didn't), and *what they'd do differently tomorrow*.

---

## 1. Business case study

Best for: founder interviews, company histories, pivots, pricing changes, market entry, failures.

| Slide | Question it answers | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Situation | Where were they? Market, size, constraints | Use a stat strip if the video gives numbers |
| Complication | What changed or went wrong? | Make it feel urgent |
| The Decision | What did they choose, and what did they give up? | Show options as a comparison if the video mentions alternatives |
| Execution | How did they actually do it? | Numbered steps, each with a timestamp |
| Outcome | What happened? | Big-number slide only if the number is SAID |
| Lessons | What transfers to other businesses? | Analysis badge |
| Discussion | "What would you have done?" | Great for classrooms and MBA-style decks |

## 2. Marketing case study

Best for: campaign breakdowns, launches, brand stories, positioning, distribution tactics, creator/influencer strategies.

| Slide | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| The Goal | What were they trying to achieve? |
| The Audience | Who exactly were they talking to? |
| The Insight | What truth about the audience unlocked the idea? |
| The Big Idea | The campaign or positioning in one line |
| Execution | Channels, creative, timing |
| Results | Reach, revenue, signups, as stated |
| Takeaways | What other marketers can reuse |

## 3. UX / product case study

Best for: design walkthroughs, redesigns, onboarding flows, research talks, product-manager interviews, portfolio-style breakdowns.

| Slide | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| The Problem | What was broken for users, and how did they know? |
| The Users | Who struggled, and in what context? |
| Research | What did they learn, and how? |
| Design Moves | 2–4 specific decisions, each its own slide |
| Before / After | Side-by-side comparison |
| Impact | Metrics or qualitative outcomes, as stated |
| Principles | Reusable design principles (analysis badge) |

## 4. Growth teardown (growth.design style)

Best for: deconstructing why a product converts or retains (paywalls, streaks, referral loops, onboarding, notifications, cancellation flows).

| Slide | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| The Hook | The surprising mechanic in one line |
| Step-by-step | Walk the user's journey screen by screen (numbered steps) |
| The Psychology | Name the principle at work (loss aversion, commitment, social proof, endowed progress, etc.) and explain it in one sentence |
| What to Steal | Concrete moves a reader can copy this week |
| The Dark Side | Where this crosses into a dark pattern, if relevant (analysis badge) |

Common psychology principles to name (only when the video supports them): loss aversion, endowed progress effect, commitment and consistency, social proof, scarcity, anchoring, default effect, reciprocity, peak-end rule, Zeigarnik effect, IKEA effect, variable rewards.

---

## Choosing when it's ambiguous

- The video is mostly **numbers and decisions** → Business
- The video is mostly **audience and message** → Marketing
- The video is mostly **screens, flows, and users** → UX / Product
- The video is mostly **"why does this app make me do X"** → Growth teardown

When a video mixes angles, pick the one that gives the strongest hook slide.
